Sixth & seventh documents — real quantitative methods, one formula-sign nuance

Two more downloaded documents proposed six predictive mathematical models (learning curves, Markov capacity states, mean-reverting commodity pricing, additive-failure logistic regression, tool-wear degradation, build-vs-buy scale economics) and a quantitative risk-management framework (Cost-FMEA/CRPN scoring, Manufacturing Value at Risk, Monte Carlo should-cost). Seven specific mathematical claims were checked independently — five came back fully correct: Wright's/Crawford's learning-curve models and the b = ln(φ)/ln(2) formula; the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck mean-reversion process (the same equation underlying the Vasicek interest-rate model); the Wiener-process-degradation → Inverse Gaussian remaining-useful-life relationship (confirmed via published cutting-tool-wear reliability literature); the Beta-PERT mean/σ approximation formulas; and the one-tailed VaR Z-scores (1.645 / 2.326). Two needed a closer look: the document's Manufacturing Value at Risk formula (M-VaRα = μ + Z·σ) looks backwards next to textbook portfolio VaR, which subtracts Z·σ — but that textbook convention applies to a RETURNS distribution where a loss is negative. This document's variance metric (Δ) is already signed the other way — positive means unfavorable — so addition is actually the correct operation for this metric's own sign convention, not an error to silently copy or silently "fix" against the wrong precedent. And Theil's Inequality Coefficient is real and correctly bounded [0,1], but the claim that 1 means "as bad as a naive forecast" belongs to a different, unrelated formula (Theil's U2) — U1 (the one actually given) is a documented poor discriminator of forecast quality and isn't built here. Separately, "Cost-FMEA" and "CRPN" are not established named frameworks — the underlying practice (classic FMEA's Probability × Severity × Detection Risk Priority Number, with dollar-banded severity) is real, but that specific branding is the document's own, labeled as such rather than implied as a citable standard. The risk register's 10 CRPN scores (P × S × D) all reproduce exactly by hand. The recurring "1,520 test fixtures" figure and the unconfirmed Project Kuiper/Amazon Robotics/AWS client relationship both appear again — same exclusion treatment, and all such references were genericized in the built modules below.

A stress-test of the navigation overhaul itself

The vertical-navigation round above shipped the most consequential interaction change on this page, so it got a dedicated adversarial review afterward: independent self-review plus a fresh-context reviewer, every finding empirically reproduced before being accepted, and every fix re-verified rather than just re-asserted. Findings, all fixed: a real 375px horizontal-scroll bug from a minmax(320px,1fr) grid track that can't shrink below its own floor; collapsed nav items and three footer controls with no real accessible name — only a CSS-hidden label or a ::after tooltip, neither in the accessibility tree; a collapse toggle that kept claiming "expanded, clickable" below the CSS's own 760px hard floor while the nav silently rendered collapsed underneath it; neither modal moving focus in on open, trapping Tab, or restoring focus on close; two stale count self-contradictions in README.md/UX_ROADMAP.md; dead CSS; a Command Palette with no ARIA combobox/listbox semantics for its arrow-key-highlighted results; and no prefers-reduced-motion support. One item was judged moderate, not broken, and is an accepted limitation rather than a fix: on touch devices, the collapsed nav's ::after tooltips only reveal on hover/focus, so a touch user sees an icon-only button with no on-screen label until they activate it — the real accessible name still exists for a screen reader either way. Two more surfaced while fixing the others, not in the original pass: live-verifying the grid fix at 375px found the Methodology tab's own long inline <code> identifiers had no wrap opportunity, overflowing the page by 13px even after the grid itself was fixed — overflow-wrap:anywhere closed it. And writing the test for the modal-focus fix found that opening a second modal while one was already open (Explain, then ⌘K for the palette) overwrote the return-focus target with the first modal's own closing button instead of the real pre-modal trigger, stranding focus once the second modal closed — fixed by only capturing the return-focus target when no modal was already open. Same discipline as every prior round: the fix isn't the deliverable, the re-verified fix is — see stress.cjs for the checks written specifically to prove each one, not just that nothing crashed.

A research pass on the 30-scenario Playbook's own KPIs — one real miscalibration found and fixed

Every one of the Cost Diagnostic Playbook's 30 KPIs was cross-checked against published industry evidence (7 independent research passes, each requiring ≥2 corroborating sources and a disconfirming search before accepting a figure). The headline finding, true across nearly all 30: the underlying concepts are overwhelmingly real, named disciplines — standard-costing variance analysis, machine-hour-rate costing, TPM/OEE, SMED, Activity-Based Costing (Cooper & Kaplan's 1988 Harvard Business Review founding argument, which the Asset Capital Burden Index KPI directly implements), should-cost modeling (a codified DFARS acquisition practice), and GAAP units-of-production depreciation — but the specific green/amber/red numeric threshold bands are, with two exceptions, internal calibrations with no traceable external benchmark. This isn't unique to this dashboard: multiple sources (including a veteran Modern Machine Shop columnist on spindle utilization) state outright that most shop-floor cost thresholds are legitimately shop-specific, not universal — so read every threshold on this page as "this build's working target," not "an audited industry standard," unless a scenario says otherwise. One KPI's thresholds were confirmed genuinely miscalibrated against real data and corrected here: the Buy-to-Fly / Swarf-to-Solid Ratio's original ≤4.0:1 "green" ceiling sat below the low end of every published aerospace-titanium buy-to-fly range found (real figures commonly run 6:1–20:1+, averaging ~11:1) — widened to ≤10.0:1 / 10.1–16.0:1 / >16.0:1 to bracket the real average with headroom for genuinely poor outliers. By contrast, First-Pass Sheet Nesting Yield's thresholds (≥85% / 75–84.9% / <75%) matched published fabrication-industry benchmarks almost exactly and needed no change. Two areas came back genuinely contradictory across sources rather than settled — metal-powder reuse rates and metal-AM build-failure rates — reported as unresolved rather than forced to a single number. Full findings, source-by-source, are filed in the vault dated 2026-09-05.

Pathways B and C — a structural rebuild and a novel view, from the same research

The research above also surfaced a structural finding: SPC/control-chart literature is explicit that a specification-limit band (a fixed policy threshold, like the green/amber/red bands throughout the Playbook) and a statistically-derived control-limit band (this shop's own actual historical variation) are two different things, and conflating them is a documented anti-pattern. Real standard-cost variance also requires posted, settled transactions — a "real-time cost variance" claim should be treated skeptically, even from an ERP vendor's own marketing. Both findings are now visible, not just documented: the Variance Waterfall was rewritten from a grouped bar chart (every bar floating from a shared zero) into a genuine cascading bridge chart, and carries a new "financial cadence — period-close, not real-time" badge. The Capacity tab gained a real Individuals (I) control chart on its existing 6-week utilization data — center line and control limits computed from this shop's own variation via the standard I-MR method, not fabricated history — and carries an "operational cadence" badge. With only 6 points the control limits are wide (a real small-sample artifact, stated on the card, not hidden), and neither Week 3 nor Week 4's fixed-band "RED" reading is actually outside those limits — a concrete illustration of exactly the distinction the research flagged. Separately, the research surfaced three real cross-KPI tradeoffs the Playbook's 30 independent scenario cards don't otherwise surface (recoater time vs. post-processing labor; powder reuse vs. build-failure risk; nesting yield vs. cycle time) — the first two, both ends real Playbook scenarios, are now a small KPI Interaction Map on the Playbook tab, each box clickable straight to that scenario; the third has no second Playbook KPI to pair against, so it's a caption, not a forced third box.
